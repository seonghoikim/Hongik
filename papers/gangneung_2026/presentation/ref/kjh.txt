I.   RESEARCH BACKGROUND – The Crucial Need for Validating AI Software
• Why is AI Software Hard to Validate?
Unlike traditional software, validating AI (especially RL) is complex due to its inherent 
characteristics:
1. Probabilistic & Non-Deterministic
2. Black Box Problem
3. Data-Driven Logic
4. Dynamic & Adaptive
5. Complex Environments
6. Ambiguous "Correctness"

To address these limitations, we propose a Scenario-based Modeling in AI Software 
Validation mechanism.
This proposed approach is based on four key theories
• State Machine Diagrams (SMDs).
• Markov Decision Processes (MDPs).
• Stochastic State Decision Processes (SSDPs).
• Scenario-based Integration Testing (Kim’99).
This approach offers an effective method to systematically validate all available 
scenarios and prioritize scenarios for AI Software and increase testing productivity.

• A mathematical framework for sequential 
decision-making, forming the theoretical 
foundation for reinforcement learning.
• Consists of States (S), Actions (A), 
Transition Probabilities (P), Rewards (R), 
and a Discount Factor (γ).
MDP = (S, A, P, R, γ), s∈S, a∈A, (0≤γ≤1)

III. Scenario-based Model (SBM) 
• In formula (11) represents the immediate reward or 
penalty expected when performing an action (at) in the 
current state (st).
• Specifically, actions leading to the goal state (Sgoals) 
are assigned a positive reward (+), and actions leading 
to the failure state (Sfails) are assigned a negative 
reward (penalty, -).
• In formula (12) represents γ stands for the discount 
factor which is an integer between -1 and 1, excluding 0.
• A positive discount rate means that future rewards 
have positive value. The larger γ, the more important 
the future is.
• 0 represents the immediate reward that does not consider 
future rewards at all.
• -1 represents as a "penalty" or "cost".
• In formula (13) represents the product of 
the probability of the path A→B (PAB) 
and the sum of the reward of the current 
state (RA) and the discounted reward of 
the next state (γRB).

Step 1. Creation AI Software Scenario for Testing 
•
Each scenario describes the process of achieving the 
system's functional goals and visually represents the 
interactions between the system and its external 
environment through a diagram.

•
Refine scenarios generated in Step 1 using decision tables, 
based on logical branching points and interaction

This helps to structure the system's internal decision-

Step 3. Convert Scenario-based Tree with Graph
•
Refine scenarios into a tree structure with common 
starting points and branching points.
•
Each node in the tree represents an RL agent's 'state'.
•
Each edge represents an 'action' between agent states.
•
Use probabilistic methods to specify frequency-based 
probability values on edges for actions, clearly 
indicating the likelihood of an agent's action selection in 
an uncertain environment.
•
This tree structure visually clarifies the various action 
sequences and their outcomes an RL system can perform. 
It also helps optimize RL-based AI software testing by 
identifying the importance of each action choice.

IV. Conclusion
Proposed approach
•
This paper introduced a Scenario-Based Model (SBM) validation mechanism for AI software, aiming to 
overcome current validation challenges. Our proposed mechanism defines an SBM by integrating concepts 
from Markov Decision Processes and Scenario-based Integration Testing.
Key features of our approach include:
•
We explicit classification of state sets into initial, intermediate, goal, and even failure states.
•
We extend the discount factor (γ) to integers for specialized validation modeling.
•
We anticipate this mechanism will significantly enhance the AI software validation process by:
•
Developing automation techniques to reduce manual effort. 
•
Establishing coverage measurement metrics specifically for AI software validation.
Ultimately, we hope our method provides a fundamental contribution to the field of AI software validation.
